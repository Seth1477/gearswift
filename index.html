Where the time goes on a new gear

  1. Weeks 1–2Certified steelGear steels in the right bar size, with mill test reports.
  2. Weeks 1–3Tooling & fixturesWorkholding built for your gear. Special hobs or cutters ordered if needed.
  3. Weeks 3–8MachiningTurning, hobbing or shaping, plus time in the queue between operations.
  4. Week 9Heat treatDone by a specialist heat treater, with a cert for hardness and case depth.
  5. Weeks 10–11GrindingBore, faces and teeth ground back to size after hardening.
  6. Weeks 11–12InspectionGear analyzer checks and the documentation package.

Actual machine time is often only days. Most of the calendar is spent waiting on steel, tooling and outside processes, which is why repeat orders and pre-hard gears are so much faster.

How it's made

CNC at every step, from blank to inspection

CNC means the machines are computer-controlled. A precision gear passes through several of them, each doing a different job.

  1. Turning & blankingA CNC lathe shapes the blank: outside diameter, bore and faces, before any teeth exist.
  2. Gear hobbingThe main way teeth are cut. A worm-shaped hob turns in sync with the blank. Fast and accurate for spur and helical gears.
  3. Gear shapingA reciprocating cutter for teeth a hob can't reach, such as internal gears and gears next to a shoulder.
  4. Heat treatmentCarburizing or through-hardening for strength and wear life, with hardness verified after.
  5. Gear grindingHardening distorts the part slightly, so the teeth are ground back to precise shape. This is how high accuracy grades are reached.
  6. Thread & ID/OD grindingWorms, threads, bores and diameters ground to tight tolerances so the gear runs true on its shaft.
  7. Gear inspectionA CNC gear analyzer measures profile, lead, pitch and runout and reports the accuracy grade achieved.
